# DroneX — Product Context

## Product Purpose
DroneX is a Malaysian drone solutions and aerial entertainment company seeking a partnership with AirAsia. The website must convey institutional authority, technical precision, and enterprise readiness to decision-makers at a major aviation group.

## Register
brand

## Users
**Primary:** AirAsia executives, procurement and innovation leads evaluating a drone solutions partner. High-trust decision-makers who fly first class and work in boardrooms. They expect the same quality signal from a vendor website that they'd see from McKinsey or DJI Enterprise.

**Secondary:** Malaysian industrial clients — plantation companies (FELDA, Sime Darby), public sector agencies (JBPM, PDRM), construction and solar companies. Evaluate vendors on credibility and compliance.

**Tertiary:** Event producers and brand managers commissioning drone shows. Responding to emotional appeal and spectacle.

## Brand
- Name: Drone X (styled as one word "DroneX" in code, displayed as "Drone X")
- Logo mark: Two curved wing strokes forming an X — aviation-elegant, not techy. The curves evoke flight paths, bird wings, propeller arcs.
- The X mark is the soul of the brand. Every page should feel like it belongs to the company that drew those two strokes.
- Palette: Dark Navy #151F27 (primary ground), Sky Blue #9DC1F5 (precision accent), Slate Gray #495F77 (midtone), Cloud #E8ECEF (light surface), White #FFFFFF, Black #000000
- Typography: Geist for display and body. JetBrains Mono for operational data, coordinates, labels, codes.
- Certifications: CAAM RPAS-A1. This is regulatory proof. Feature it as a credential, not a footnote.

## Tone
Authoritative. Precise. Minimal. Never playful or startup-casual. Think: aviation safety brief meets editorial design annual. Every word earns its place. No exclamation marks. No "cutting-edge" or "revolutionary."

## Anti-references
- No SaaS dashboard aesthetic (metric tiles, progress rings, activity feeds)
- No glassmorphism cards as decoration
- No gradient text
- No startup landing page energy (emoji, informal CTAs, "Let's chat!")
- No drone photography stock clichés (sunset drone shots, generic aerial grids)
- No fintech navy-and-gold
- No generic "hero with big number stats" template

## Strategic Principles
1. The AirAsia meeting must be won on this site. Every design decision is evaluated: "Would a VP at AirAsia think this is a serious company?"
2. Credibility through specificity: coordinates, certification codes, flight numbers, waypoint IDs. Real operational detail over marketing language.
3. The X emblem is a structural element, not decoration. Use it at scale as a spatial anchor.
4. Section rhythm creates the argument: from aerial authority (hero) to company identity (about) to evidence (services, specs) to trust (partners, CTA).
5. Motion serves information, never entertainment.
